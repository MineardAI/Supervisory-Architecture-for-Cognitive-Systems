# SACS Contract 012 — External Verification Profile and Evidence Interface

## Section 10 — Verification Scope and Applicability

### Purpose

Section 10 defines the constitutional gate that determines whether a proposed external verification operation may proceed.

This section evaluates whether one exact:

* verification subject;
* Verifiable Claim Set;
* Verification Profile;
* Verification Requirement Set;
* Verification Method Set;
* external verifier;
* implementation scope;
* environment scope;
* temporal scope;
* version scope;

form a constitutionally valid and operationally resolvable verification context.

The purpose of applicability evaluation is not to determine whether a claim is true.

Its purpose is to determine whether the claim is sufficiently authorized, bounded, supported, and compatible to be submitted for external verification.

No evidence package SHALL be constructed and no Verification Submission SHALL be committed until applicability has been evaluated.

---

# Constitutional Question

Section 10 answers:

> **Given one proposed verification operation, is external verification constitutionally authorized and technically meaningful for the exact subject, claims, profile, requirements, methods, verifier, scope, versions, implementation, and environment presented?**

The answer SHALL be represented through exactly one canonical:

```text
Verification Applicability Result
```

Applicability SHALL be determined before substantive evidence evaluation by the external verifier begins.

---

# Constitutional Identity

The defining constitutional identity of verification applicability is:

> **Verification applicability is the deterministic determination that one bounded verification operation is authorized, internally coherent, sufficiently specified, and compatible with its governing profile, requirements, methods, verifier, subject, claims, versions, implementation, environment, and scope.**

Applicability is not:

* claim verification;
* evidence sufficiency judgment by the external verifier;
* verifier success;
* implementation certification;
* historical certification;
* governance approval;
* deployment authorization.

Formally:

```text
Applicable
≠
Verified
```

and:

```text
Not Applicable
≠
Not Verified
```

---

# Applicability Authority

Contract 012 owns applicability evaluation.

Contract 012 MAY determine:

* whether the subject is eligible;
* whether the claims are structurally valid;
* whether the Verification Profile is authorized;
* whether the Requirement Set is coherent;
* whether the required methods are recognized;
* whether the verifier is compatible;
* whether scope is complete and bounded;
* whether required versions are resolved;
* whether implementation identity is sufficient;
* whether environment identity is sufficient;
* whether required evidence classes are potentially resolvable;
* whether constitutional prohibitions apply.

Contract 012 SHALL NOT determine:

* whether the claims are true;
* whether the evidence proves the claims;
* whether a required method will succeed;
* whether the verifier will issue a favorable result;
* whether the system should be deployed;
* whether governance should accept the result.

# Applicability Classes

The canonical applicability classes SHALL include:

```text
APPLICABLE
APPLICABLE_WITH_RESTRICTIONS
NOT_APPLICABLE
UNSUPPORTED
INCOMPATIBLE
UNRESOLVED
PROHIBITED
```

A Verification Profile MAY define more specific subordinate reason codes.

It SHALL NOT redefine the constitutional meaning of the primary classes.

---

# Applicable

`APPLICABLE` means:

* the subject is eligible;
* all claims are valid and supported;
* the profile is authorized and compatible;
* the Requirement Set is coherent;
* mandatory methods are recognized;
* the verifier possesses required capabilities;
* scope is complete and bounded;
* required versions are resolved;
* implementation identity is sufficient;
* environment identity is sufficient;
* no constitutional prohibition applies;
* no unresolved mandatory condition remains.

`APPLICABLE` authorizes progression into evidence resolution and package construction.

It does not predict a favorable external result.

---

# Applicable With Restrictions

`APPLICABLE_WITH_RESTRICTIONS` means the operation may proceed only within explicitly narrowed or conditioned boundaries authorized by the Verification Profile.

Restrictions MAY include:

* reduced temporal scope;
* reduced environment scope;
* bounded implementation coverage;
* reduced method coverage where explicitly permitted;
* limited claim set;
* required additional evidence;
* mandatory verifier disclosure;
* restricted result vocabulary;
* mandatory human review;
* shortened validity interval;
* enhanced privacy or security handling.

Every restriction SHALL be explicit.

A restricted applicability result SHALL contain:

```text
restriction_id
restriction_type
restriction_basis
affected_claim_ids
affected_scope
required_condition
result_impact
validity_impact
```

Restrictions SHALL NOT be introduced merely to avoid an otherwise mandatory failure.

---

# Not Applicable

`NOT_APPLICABLE` means the verification request does not meaningfully apply to the presented subject, claim, scope, or purpose.

Examples include:

* a runtime-behavior claim applied to a schema-only artifact;
* a replay claim applied to a subject with no replay semantics;
* a numeric-conformance claim applied to a nonnumeric property;
* a temporal claim applied outside the subject's valid interval;
* a claim whose preconditions establish that the property is irrelevant.

`NOT_APPLICABLE` is not an adverse verification judgment.

It means the proposed verification question does not apply as framed.

---

# Unsupported

`UNSUPPORTED` means the request is constitutionally meaningful but cannot be performed under the available or authorized verification framework.

Examples include:

* no adopted Verification Profile supports the claim type;
* no recognized method supports the property;
* no authorized verifier class possesses the required capability;
* no result schema can represent the required outcome;
* the subject type is not yet supported by Contract 012.

Unsupported status SHALL remain distinct from incompatibility.

---

# Incompatible

`INCOMPATIBLE` means individually valid components cannot operate together in the proposed verification context.

Examples include:

* profile and claim incompatibility;
* method and subject incompatibility;
* verifier and method incompatibility;
* environment and implementation incompatibility;
* evidence and privacy-policy incompatibility;
* conflicting version requirements;
* conflicting requirement obligations.

An incompatible operation SHALL not proceed unless a constitutionally authorized new configuration resolves the incompatibility.

---

# Unresolved

`UNRESOLVED` means applicability cannot yet be determined because one or more mandatory facts remain unavailable, ambiguous, or unvalidated.

Examples include:

* unresolved implementation hash;
* missing environment identity;
* unavailable registry snapshot;
* unresolved verifier credential;
* ambiguous temporal boundary;
* unresolved profile status;
* incomplete version binding;
* unresolved requirement conflict.

`UNRESOLVED` SHALL not be treated as `APPLICABLE`.

Contract 012 MAY permit resolution attempts where authorized.

---

# Prohibited

`PROHIBITED` means the proposed operation violates an explicit constitutional, governance, privacy, security, trust, or authority rule.

Examples include:

* use of an unauthorized verifier;
* submission of prohibited evidence;
* verification of mutable uncommitted state where commitment is mandatory;
* use of a revoked profile;
* use of a prohibited method;
* attempt to verify an implied or universal claim;
* attempt to modify history through verification;
* attempt to combine submission construction and verdict authority;
* attempt to conceal known contradictory evidence.

A prohibited operation SHALL not proceed through restriction, fallback, or operator override unless constitutional governance changes the governing rule.

# Subject Applicability

The verification subject SHALL be eligible under Section 3 and supported by the selected Verification Profile.

Subject applicability SHALL validate:

* exact subject identity;
* exact subject type;
* subject commitment status;
* subject hash;
* subject schema;
* subject version;
* subject lineage;
* subject availability;
* subject immutability.

A subject that cannot be uniquely resolved SHALL not be applicable.

---

# Committed-Subject Requirement

Where the Verification Profile requires a committed subject, only the exact committed artifact may be used.

The following SHALL NOT substitute:

* a copy without commitment proof;
* a reconstructed artifact;
* a mutable database view;
* a human-authored summary;
* a replay output;
* an audit representation;
* an implementation approximation.

Formally:

```text
Committed Subject
≠
Representation of Committed Subject
```

---

# Composed Subjects

A Verification Profile MAY authorize a composed subject consisting of multiple committed artifacts.

A composed subject SHALL identify:

```text
composed_subject_id
primary_subject_id
component_subject_ids
composition_rule
lineage_relationships
scope_rule
composition_hash
```

Every component SHALL be individually resolvable.

The composed subject SHALL not be created by informal aggregation.

---

# Claim Applicability

Every Verifiable Claim SHALL be evaluated for applicability.

Claim applicability SHALL validate:

* claim identity;
* claim type;
* claim subject binding;
* property definition;
* scope;
* assumptions;
* preconditions;
* exclusions;
* limitations;
* dependencies;
* version bindings;
* profile support;
* method support.

One claim's applicability SHALL not establish another claim's applicability.

---

# Claim-Set Applicability

Where multiple claims are submitted together, the claim set SHALL be evaluated for:

* internal consistency;
* dependency validity;
* composition validity;
* profile compatibility;
* shared-scope compatibility;
* shared-evidence compatibility;
* method compatibility;
* conflict.

A claim set MAY be:

```text
FULLY_APPLICABLE
PARTIALLY_APPLICABLE
INCOMPATIBLE_AS_A_SET
```

A partially applicable claim set MAY proceed only where the Verification Profile permits separation or restricted submission.

---

# Claim Conflict Evaluation

Contract 012 SHALL detect materially conflicting claims.

Examples include:

* mutually contradictory assertions;
* incompatible assumptions;
* incompatible scopes;
* inconsistent subject versions;
* contradictory exclusions;
* incompatible result dependencies.

Conflict SHALL not automatically mean that either claim is false.

It means the proposed joint verification context may be incoherent.

---

# Purpose Applicability

The declared verification purpose SHALL be supported by:

* the Verification Profile;
* the claim types;
* the subject types;
* the selected methods;
* the proposed verifier.

A purpose SHALL not be inferred solely from claim wording.

A profile for structural conformance SHALL not automatically authorize stability verification.

---

# Profile Applicability

The selected Verification Profile SHALL satisfy:

* exact registry resolution;
* valid integrity;
* authorized status;
* effective interval;
* subject support;
* claim support;
* purpose support;
* verifier-class support;
* compatible scope rules;
* compatible result schema;
* compatible privacy and security rules.

An eligible profile may still be inapplicable to the exact operation.

---

# Requirement-Set Applicability

The Committed Verification Requirement Set SHALL be evaluated for:

* derivation from the exact profile;
* derivation from the exact claim set;
* derivation from the exact subject;
* internal consistency;
* complete requirement identity;
* conflict absence;
* dependency resolution;
* satisfiable mandatory obligations;
* compatible failure rules.

A Requirement Set that refers to another subject, profile, or claim set SHALL be inapplicable.

---

# Requirement Feasibility

Applicability evaluation SHALL determine whether each mandatory requirement is potentially satisfiable.

This is a feasibility determination.

It is not final requirement satisfaction.

Examples:

```text
Required evidence class exists and may be acquired
```

is a feasibility finding.

```text
The evidence proves the claim
```

is not.

---

# Method Applicability

Every required or proposed Verification Method SHALL be evaluated against:

* claim type;
* subject type;
* purpose;
* required evidence;
* assumptions;
* preconditions;
* verifier capabilities;
* execution environment;
* version requirements;
* privacy constraints;
* security constraints.

Method applicability SHALL not imply method success.

---

# Method-Set Applicability

Where multiple methods are required, the method set SHALL be checked for:

* composition validity;
* execution ordering;
* dependency resolution;
* shared-input compatibility;
* shared-evidence compatibility;
* environment compatibility;
* verifier-capability compatibility;
* aggregate interpretation rules.

A valid individual method MAY be incompatible with the complete method set.

---

# Verifier Applicability

The proposed external verifier SHALL be evaluated for:

* exact identity;
* verifier class;
* authorization status;
* credentials;
* required capabilities;
* method support;
* subject support;
* claim support;
* environment support;
* privacy compliance;
* security compliance;
* trust-domain separation;
* result-schema support.

The constitutional verifier relationship established in Section 5 SHALL remain preserved.

---

# Verifier Independence

Where the Verification Profile requires verifier independence, applicability evaluation SHALL validate the required separation.

Independence MAY require separation from:

* the subject-producing implementation;
* Contract 011 historical commitment;
* Contract 012 submission construction;
* deployment authority;
* governance authority;
* prior method execution;
* evidence generation.

Technical co-location SHALL not automatically defeat independence.

Constitutional authority separation SHALL be demonstrable.

---

# Verifier Credential Validity

Verifier credentials SHALL be valid for:

* the verifier identity;
* the method class;
* the claim class;
* the submission period;
* the required trust domain.

Expired, revoked, ambiguous, or unsupported credentials SHALL prevent applicable status where credential validity is mandatory.

---

# Evidence Resolvability

Applicability evaluation SHALL determine whether required evidence classes are potentially available and constitutionally admissible.

The evaluation SHALL consider:

* evidence source;
* evidence existence;
* evidence commitment state;
* evidence accessibility;
* evidence authority;
* evidence version;
* evidence temporal scope;
* evidence privacy constraints;
* evidence security constraints;
* evidence integrity mechanism.

Evidence resolvability is not evidence completeness.

---

# Evidence Availability Classes

Evidence availability MAY be classified as:

```text
AVAILABLE
RESOLVABLE
CONDITIONALLY_RESOLVABLE
UNAVAILABLE
PROHIBITED
UNKNOWN
```

A mandatory evidence requirement classified as `UNAVAILABLE`, `PROHIBITED`, or unresolved `UNKNOWN` SHALL prevent unrestricted applicability.

---

# Scope Applicability

Every verification operation SHALL possess one complete Scope Declaration.

The Scope Declaration SHALL identify:

```text
subject_scope
claim_scope
artifact_scope
contract_scope
transformation_scope
temporal_scope
version_scope
implementation_scope
environment_scope
evidence_scope
method_scope
verifier_scope
result_scope
```

All scope dimensions SHALL be mutually consistent.

---

# Scope Closure

Scope is closed when every material boundary is explicit and no required element depends upon an unstated external domain.

A scope SHALL fail closure where it relies upon terms such as:

```text
all relevant artifacts
normal operation
current deployment
usual configuration
standard environment
sufficient history
```

unless those terms resolve deterministically through an adopted profile rule.

---

# Scope Narrowing

A proposed scope MAY be narrowed before applicability commitment where:

* the narrowing authority is valid;
* the change is explicit;
* affected claims receive new identities where required;
* affected requirements are regenerated;
* no known contradictory evidence is concealed;
* the profile permits the narrowed scope.

Scope SHALL not be narrowed after external verification begins without creating a new submission.

---

# Scope Expansion

Scope expansion SHALL require:

* new or revised claims;
* new applicability evaluation;
* new Requirement Set where material;
* new evidence obligations;
* new submission identity.

A result SHALL never be expanded beyond its submitted scope.

---

# Temporal Applicability

Temporal applicability SHALL validate:

* exact interval identity;
* subject existence during the interval;
* evidence coverage;
* version validity;
* profile validity;
* verifier authorization;
* environment identity;
* implementation identity.

Temporal scope SHALL not be established through ambiguous relative language.

---

# Point-in-Time and Interval Claims

A point-in-time claim SHALL identify one exact time or committed event boundary.

An interval claim SHALL identify:

* interval start;
* interval end;
* inclusion rules;
* applicable subject versions;
* environment transitions;
* implementation transitions;
* evidence coverage.

An interval crossing a material version or configuration change SHALL be split or explicitly modeled.

---

# Historical Applicability

A historical verification operation MAY use a profile or method no longer active for new submissions only where:

* historical replay or retrospective verification is explicitly authorized;
* the profile and method remain resolvable;
* their status permits historical use;
* the result clearly states retrospective scope;
* no current authorization is implied.

Historical applicability SHALL not reactivate revoked authority for new operational use.

---

# Version Applicability

Every material version SHALL be resolved and compatible.

Applicable versions MAY include:

* contract versions;
* artifact schema versions;
* profile version;
* Requirement Set version;
* method versions;
* registry snapshots;
* property-definition versions;
* implementation version;
* runtime version;
* numeric profile version;
* cryptographic profile version.

Unresolved version references SHALL prevent applicable status.

---

# Version Compatibility

Version compatibility SHALL be established only through:

* exact version equality;
* adopted compatibility mapping;
* deterministic migration rule;
* profile-authorized equivalence rule.

Similarity of version labels SHALL not establish compatibility.

---

# Mixed-Version Verification

A Verification Profile MAY authorize mixed-version verification.

The operation SHALL identify:

* every version boundary;
* compatibility rules;
* migration transformations;
* evidence transformations;
* affected claims;
* affected methods;
* result limitations.

Mixed-version verification SHALL not silently treat all artifacts as one version.

---

# Implementation Applicability

Where the claim concerns an implementation, applicability SHALL validate:

* implementation identity;
* source revision;
* build identity;
* binary hash;
* dependency manifest;
* configuration;
* deployment identity;
* runtime identity;
* profile conformance status where required.

An implementation claim SHALL not apply to an unresolved or mutable implementation identity.

---

# Build and Deployment Distinction

The distinction SHALL remain:

```text
Implementation Build
≠
Deployment Instance
```

A build-level verification result SHALL not automatically apply to every deployment.

A deployment-level claim SHALL identify the deployment-specific configuration and environment.

---

# Configuration Applicability

Where implementation behavior depends upon configuration, the exact configuration SHALL be bound.

A result concerning one configuration SHALL not apply to another unless the Verification Profile defines an explicit equivalence rule.

Mutable configuration references such as:

```text
production settings
default configuration
current values
```

SHALL not establish canonical configuration identity.

---

# Environment Applicability

Environment applicability SHALL validate every environmental condition material to the claim or methods.

This MAY include:

* hardware;
* operating system;
* runtime;
* container;
* dependency set;
* network;
* timing source;
* resource limits;
* random source;
* secure enclave;
* external service versions;
* deployment region.

Environment-independent claims SHALL require explicit profile authorization.

---

# Environment Equivalence

Two environments may be considered equivalent only through an adopted environment-equivalence rule.

The rule SHALL define:

* relevant dimensions;
* permitted variation;
* prohibited variation;
* comparison procedure;
* equivalence evidence;
* result limitations.

Informal similarity SHALL not establish equivalence.

---

# Authority Applicability

Every verification operation SHALL identify the authority for:

* claim proposal;
* claim authorization;
* profile selection;
* requirement construction;
* method binding;
* verifier designation;
* evidence access;
* submission commitment.

Missing authority SHALL prevent applicability.

Contract 012 SHALL not infer authority from possession of data or technical capability.

---

# Governance Constraints

Applicability evaluation SHALL enforce all applicable governance constraints.

These MAY include:

* prohibited claim classes;
* required independent review;
* restricted subject classes;
* mandatory verifier classes;
* privacy requirements;
* national or regional restrictions;
* regulatory requirements;
* evidence-retention limits;
* mandatory escalation.

Governance constraints SHALL not be silently overridden by a technically compatible profile.

---

# Privacy Applicability

Privacy applicability SHALL determine whether the proposed evidence and methods comply with the applicable privacy rules.

It SHALL evaluate:

* data classification;
* disclosure authority;
* redaction rules;
* pseudonymization rules;
* minimum-necessary access;
* data residency;
* retention;
* destruction;
* verifier access;
* downstream reporting.

Privacy restrictions SHALL not be treated as optional implementation preferences.

---

# Privacy-Preserving Applicability

Where direct evidence disclosure is prohibited, a profile MAY authorize privacy-preserving evidence forms such as:

* cryptographic commitments;
* zero-knowledge proofs;
* redacted records;
* secure enclave attestations;
* aggregate evidence;
* controlled reviewer access.

The substitute evidence form SHALL preserve the property necessary to evaluate the claim.

---

# Security Applicability

Security applicability SHALL evaluate:

* evidence-channel security;
* verifier authentication;
* submission integrity;
* toolchain integrity;
* execution isolation;
* key management;
* tamper evidence;
* access control;
* result transmission;
* audit preservation.

Failure of a mandatory security requirement SHALL prevent applicable status.

---

# Trust-Model Applicability

Every verification operation SHALL identify its trust model where material.

The trust model SHALL define:

* trusted authorities;
* trusted tools;
* trusted roots;
* trusted environments;
* untrusted components;
* verifier independence assumptions;
* cryptographic assumptions;
* human authority assumptions.

A result SHALL remain bounded by the trust model under which applicability was established.

---

# Threat-Model Applicability

Where claims concern security, resilience, or adversarial behavior, the threat model SHALL be applicable to:

* the subject;
* the claimed property;
* the environment;
* the methods;
* the verifier capabilities.

A result under one threat model SHALL not apply to a stronger or materially different threat model.

---

# Fault-Model Applicability

Where claims concern fault tolerance or recovery, applicability SHALL validate:

* fault types;
* fault cardinality;
* injection boundaries;
* temporal assumptions;
* recovery criteria;
* environment assumptions.

An unstated fault model SHALL not support a fault-tolerance claim.

---

# Applicability Restrictions

Restrictions SHALL be used only where:

* the Verification Profile explicitly authorizes them;
* they preserve claim meaning or create a new claim identity;
* they do not conceal mandatory failure;
* they are fully represented;
* their impact on result interpretation is explicit.

Restrictions MAY NOT:

* convert prohibited into applicable;
* convert unsupported into verified;
* remove mandatory contradictory evidence;
* silently weaken a claim;
* bypass verifier independence;
* bypass governance authority.

---

# Applicability Conditions

An applicability result MAY include conditions that SHALL be satisfied before evidence package commitment.

Conditions MAY include:

* resolve one registry snapshot;
* obtain one verifier credential;
* provide one missing implementation hash;
* satisfy one privacy-control obligation;
* complete one required environment binding;
* separate incompatible claims;
* resolve one requirement conflict.

The result SHALL state whether the condition is:

```text
PRE_COMMITMENT_REQUIRED
PRE_SUBMISSION_REQUIRED
PRE_METHOD_EXECUTION_REQUIRED
RESULT_LIMITING
```

---

# Applicability and Evidence Sufficiency

Contract 012 SHALL preserve the distinction:

```text
Evidence Potentially Resolvable
≠
Evidence Sufficient to Verify
```

Applicability may determine that evidence obligations can be met.

Only the external verification process may determine whether the submitted evidence is substantively sufficient under the applicable methods and claim.

---

# Applicability and Preconditions

Claim and method preconditions SHALL be classified as:

```text
SATISFIED_BEFORE_SUBMISSION
EVALUATED_BY_VERIFIER
CONDITIONALLY_ASSUMED
UNRESOLVED
FAILED
```

The Verification Profile SHALL determine which class is permitted for each precondition.

A mandatory precondition that must be satisfied before submission SHALL not be deferred to the verifier.

---

# Partial Applicability

A verification operation MAY be partially applicable only where the Verification Profile explicitly permits:

* claim separation;
* scope reduction;
* method reduction;
* evidence partition;
* staged submission.

Partial applicability SHALL identify every excluded claim, scope, method, or requirement.

An excluded element SHALL not receive an implied result.

---

# Staged Applicability

A Verification Profile MAY define staged verification.

Example:

```text
Stage One
Structural and integrity applicability

↓

Stage Two
Method and evidence applicability

↓

Stage Three
External verification submission
```

Each stage SHALL possess explicit entry and exit criteria.

Stage completion SHALL not imply later-stage success.

---

# Applicability Re-Evaluation

Applicability SHALL be re-evaluated when any material input changes.

Material changes include:

* subject identity;
* claim identity;
* profile binding;
* Requirement Set;
* method set;
* verifier identity;
* scope;
* version;
* implementation;
* configuration;
* environment;
* governance rule;
* privacy rule;
* security rule;
* trust model.

A materially changed operation SHALL require a new Applicability Result.

---

# Applicability Expiration

An Applicability Result MAY expire before submission where:

* verifier credentials expire;
* profile authority expires;
* registry status changes;
* subject availability changes;
* implementation changes;
* environment changes;
* governance rules change;
* privacy or security conditions change;
* a profile-defined validity period ends.

Expiration SHALL not delete the original result.

---

# Applicability Supersession

A later Applicability Result MAY supersede an earlier result.

Supersession SHALL identify:

```text
superseding_applicability_result_id
superseded_applicability_result_id
supersession_basis
changed_inputs
effective_time
authorizing_authority
```

The earlier result SHALL remain preserved.

---

# Deterministic Applicability Evaluation

For identical:

* subject;
* claim set;
* profile binding;
* Requirement Set;
* method bindings;
* verifier identity;
* scope;
* versions;
* implementation;
* environment;
* registry snapshots;
* governance constraints;

Contract 012 SHALL produce the same Applicability Result.

Applicability SHALL NOT depend upon:

* expected verifier outcome;
* operator preference;
* provider defaults;
* mutable aliases;
* undocumented environmental knowledge;
* anticipated deployment value.

---

# Applicability Replay

Contract 012 SHALL support deterministic replay of applicability evaluation.

Replay SHALL reproduce:

* every evaluated dimension;
* every input identity;
* every compatibility decision;
* every restriction;
* every unresolved condition;
* every failure reason;
* the final applicability class;
* applicability identity;
* applicability hash.

Replay SHALL not perform external verification.

---

# Applicability Auditability

Every Applicability Result SHALL preserve sufficient provenance to determine:

* who requested verification;
* who authorized the claims;
* which profile was selected;
* which Requirement Set applied;
* which methods were required;
* which verifier was proposed;
* which scope was evaluated;
* which versions were resolved;
* which restrictions were imposed;
* which incompatibilities were found;
* why the final class was assigned.

---

# Applicability Commitment

A Verification Applicability Result becomes authoritative only after commitment.

Before commitment:

```text
Constructed Applicability Result
≠
Committed Applicability Result
```

Commitment SHALL establish:

* exact identity;
* immutable input bindings;
* immutable findings;
* immutable applicability class;
* deterministic replay identity;
* authorization to proceed or prohibition from proceeding.

---

# Progression Rule

Only the following applicability classes MAY authorize progression:

```text
APPLICABLE
APPLICABLE_WITH_RESTRICTIONS
```

`APPLICABLE_WITH_RESTRICTIONS` MAY proceed only after all pre-progression conditions are satisfied.

The following classes SHALL not authorize evidence-package commitment:

```text
NOT_APPLICABLE
UNSUPPORTED
INCOMPATIBLE
UNRESOLVED
PROHIBITED
```

---

# No Silent Fallback

Where applicability fails, Contract 012 SHALL NOT silently:

* choose another profile;
* choose another verifier;
* remove claims;
* reduce scope;
* replace methods;
* ignore versions;
* substitute evidence;
* waive privacy or security requirements;
* infer authorization.

Any changed proposal SHALL undergo a new applicability evaluation.

---

# Failure Conditions

Applicability evaluation SHALL fail when:

* the subject is unresolved;
* the subject is ineligible;
* the subject is mutable where commitment is required;
* a claim is invalid;
* the claim set is incoherent;
* the verification purpose is unsupported;
* the Verification Profile cannot be resolved;
* the profile is unauthorized;
* the Requirement Set is inconsistent;
* a required method is unsupported;

* applicability identity cannot be committed.

Every failure SHALL be explicitly represented.

---

# Prohibitions

Contract 012 SHALL NOT:

* treat applicability as verification;
* treat inapplicability as claim falsity;
* proceed without a committed Applicability Result;
* infer missing scope;
* infer missing authority;
* use mutable subjects where commitment is required;
* use unresolved versions;
* use unsupported methods;
* use unauthorized verifiers;
* conceal requirement conflict;
* conceal claim conflict;
* narrow scope to avoid known failure without creating new authority;
* convert unresolved status into applicable status through operator discretion;
* bypass privacy, security, trust, or governance constraints;
* reuse an applicability result after a material input changes;
* allow an external result to exceed the applicable submitted scope.

---

# Section 10 Invariants

> **Every external verification operation under Contract 012 SHALL possess exactly one committed Verification Applicability Result that deterministically establishes whether the exact subject, claims, purpose, Verification Profile, Verification Requirement Set, methods, verifier, evidence obligations, scope, versions, implementation, environment, authority, privacy, security, and trust conditions are constitutionally authorized and mutually compatible before evidence-package construction begins.**

> **Only an `APPLICABLE` or condition-satisfied `APPLICABLE_WITH_RESTRICTIONS` result may authorize progression, and no applicability determination may verify a claim, predict a result, conceal a conflict, infer missing authority, or permit verification beyond the exact committed scope it evaluates.**
