⸻

1. Purpose

This contract defines the canonical supervisory objective model for SACS.

It establishes:

* objective authority;
* objective declarations;
* objective registration;
* objective applicability;
* objective governance eligibility;
* objective activation;
* objective priority;
* objective compatibility;
* objective conflict handling;
* objective satisfaction evaluation;
* objective status;
* Objective Set construction;
* deterministic objective evaluation;
* objective provenance;
* Profile Selector handoff;
* failure handling;
* conformance requirements.

Supervisory Objectives declare the operational conditions that SACS is intended to preserve, restore, maintain, approach, avoid, or bound.

Objectives do not determine how those conditions are achieved.

⸻

2. Normative Language

The key words MUST, MUST NOT, REQUIRED, SHALL, SHALL NOT, SHOULD, SHOULD NOT, RECOMMENDED, MAY, and OPTIONAL are normative conformance terms as defined by Contract 000.

⸻

3. Supervisory Objective Identity

A Supervisory Objective is a declarative, versioned, governance-eligible statement of an operational condition.

The defining identity of a Supervisory Objective is:

A Supervisory Objective declares what operational condition should be preserved or approached without specifying the controller, command, tool, model, or execution mechanism used to achieve it.

An objective MAY declare:

* a condition to preserve;
* a condition to restore;
* a condition to approach;
* a condition to avoid;
* a bounded operating region;
* a maximum or minimum acceptable state;
* a required supervisory priority;
* a stabilization requirement;
* a continuity requirement;
* a time-bounded target condition;
* a tolerance around a desired condition.

An objective MUST NOT contain:

* controller gains;
* control equations;
* inference commands;
* execution instructions;
* model-routing instructions;
* tool-call instructions;
* profile-selection algorithms;
* governance modifications;
* OSV field redefinitions;
* hidden-state inference;
* task-content instructions.

⸻

6. Objective Sources

Supervisory Objectives MAY originate from:

* a registered SACS objective profile;
* a deployment configuration;
* an authorized system owner;
* an external governance requirement;
* a safety or stabilization requirement;
* a registered operational policy;
* a capability-specific objective catalog;
* a prior-cycle continuation rule;
* a Contract 010 mode requirement when later defined.

Objective origin MUST be explicit.

Objective origin does not bypass governance eligibility.

An externally proposed objective MUST be normalized into the canonical objective representation before evaluation.

8. Objective Classes

The initial objective classes are:

PRESERVE
RESTORE
APPROACH
AVOID
BOUND
MINIMIZE
MAXIMIZE
STABILIZE
MAINTAIN_CONTINUITY
HOLD

8.1 Preserve

A PRESERVE objective declares that an acceptable operational condition should remain within its declared region.

8.2 Restore

A RESTORE objective declares that an operational condition outside its acceptable region should return to that region.

8.3 Approach

An APPROACH objective declares that the system should move toward a declared target condition or region.

8.4 Avoid

An AVOID objective declares that the system should remain outside a prohibited or undesirable operational region.

8.5 Bound

A BOUND objective declares upper, lower, or multidimensional limits on operational state.

8.6 Minimize

A MINIMIZE objective declares that a registered operational quantity should be reduced, subject to governance and higher-priority objectives.

8.7 Maximize

A MAXIMIZE objective declares that a registered operational quantity should be increased, subject to governance and higher-priority objectives.

MAXIMIZE MUST NOT imply unlimited increase.

Every MAXIMIZE objective MUST include an admissible bound, saturation rule, or governance-constrained operating region.

8.8 Stabilize

A STABILIZE objective declares that the system should move toward or remain within a registered stabilization region.

STABILIZE does not select the Stabilize operational mode.

Mode selection belongs to later contracts.

8.9 Maintain Continuity

A MAINTAIN_CONTINUITY objective declares that a registered continuity condition should be preserved across cycles.

8.10 Hold

A HOLD objective declares that a state, reference condition, authority level, or supervisory configuration should remain unchanged within declared tolerance.

10. Declarative Target Conditions

A target condition MUST be expressed using registered OSV fields or registered composite state predicates.

A target condition MAY be:

equality target
bounded interval
upper bound
lower bound
multidimensional region
categorical state requirement
set-membership requirement
monotonic direction
rate-of-change condition
temporal persistence condition
logical composition of registered predicates

Examples include:

osv.stability >= 0.80
osv.strain <= 0.35
osv.uncertainty within [0.00, 0.25]
osv.execution == AVAILABLE
osv.continuity remains VALID for N cycles

These examples are illustrative only until the referenced OSV fields are normatively registered under Contract 003.

An objective MUST NOT create a private interpretation of an OSV field.

12. OSV Validity and Objective Evaluation

An objective MAY depend only on OSV fields declared by its registry entry.

Before evaluating an objective, the Objective Evaluator MUST inspect:

* field presence;
* field validity;
* field uncertainty;
* field quality;
* schema compatibility;
* registry compatibility;
* publication status.

A numeric OSV value MUST NOT be treated as usable solely because it is present.

An objective evaluation rule MUST define behavior for:

VALID
DEGRADED
UNRESOLVED
UNAVAILABLE
INCOMPATIBLE
REDACTED

An unresolved or unavailable required field MUST NOT be silently interpreted as objective satisfaction.

⸻

13. Objective Applicability

An objective is applicable only when its registered applicability rule evaluates true.

Applicability MAY depend on:

* system identity;
* deployment identity;
* capability profile;
* committed OSV;
* Governance Snapshot;
* effective time;
* prior Objective Set;
* session state represented canonically;
* external authorization represented in governance;
* registered supervisory context.

Applicability MUST NOT depend on unregistered hidden context.

Each candidate objective MUST resolve to one applicability state:

APPLICABLE
NOT_APPLICABLE
APPLICABILITY_UNRESOLVED
APPLICABILITY_INCOMPATIBLE

An objective with unresolved applicability MUST NOT be activated as nominal.

⸻

14. Governance Eligibility

Every applicable objective MUST be evaluated against the committed Governance Snapshot.

Governance MAY:

* permit the objective;
* prohibit the objective;
* require the objective;
* restrict its scope;
* bound its target;
* alter its maximum priority within authorized limits;
* require review;
* require escalation;
* require stabilization-compatible behavior;
* require additional evidence.

Contract 005 MUST NOT modify the Governance Snapshot.

A prohibited objective MUST NOT enter the active Objective Set.

A required objective MUST be included when applicable unless doing so creates an unresolved higher-precedence governance conflict.

Governance eligibility MUST resolve to one of:

ELIGIBLE
ELIGIBLE_RESTRICTED
REQUIRED
PROHIBITED
DEFERRED
REVIEW_REQUIRED
ESCALATION_REQUIRED
UNRESOLVED

15. Objective Status

Every evaluated objective MUST declare one status:

INACTIVE
CANDIDATE
ACTIVE
ACTIVE_RESTRICTED
SATISFIED
VIOLATED
AT_RISK
DEFERRED
BLOCKED
CONFLICTED
UNRESOLVED
EXPIRED
SUPERSEDED

15.1 Inactive

The objective exists but is not currently applicable or selected for inclusion.

15.2 Candidate

The objective is applicable and under evaluation for inclusion.

15.3 Active

The objective is included in the committed Objective Set.

15.4 Active Restricted

The objective is included but narrowed by governance.

15.5 Satisfied

The current OSV satisfies the objective according to its satisfaction rule.

A satisfied objective MAY remain active when its persistence policy requires preservation.

15.6 Violated

The current OSV violates a mandatory target condition or bound.

15.7 At Risk

The objective is not yet violated, but its registered risk condition is satisfied.

15.8 Deferred

The objective cannot currently be activated because required conditions or evidence are absent.

15.9 Blocked

Governance prohibits activation.

15.10 Conflicted

The objective is incompatible with one or more simultaneously applicable objectives and the conflict has not been fully resolved.

15.11 Unresolved

The objective cannot be evaluated because its canonical inputs are unavailable, invalid, or incompatible.

15.12 Expired

The objective’s validity period has ended.

15.13 Superseded

A newer registered objective or objective instance replaces it.

16. Objective Satisfaction

Every objective MUST define a deterministic satisfaction rule.

A satisfaction rule MUST include:

satisfaction_rule_id

predicate
tolerance
uncertainty_treatment
validity_requirements
quality_requirements
temporal_requirement

failure_behavior
schema_version
registry_version

Satisfaction MAY be:

SATISFIED
PARTIALLY_SATISFIED
AT_RISK
VIOLATED
UNRESOLVED

Objective satisfaction is an assessment of current operational condition.

It is not a control command.

It does not determine the active profile by itself.

17. Objective Error Is Not Control Error

Contract 005 MAY compute declarative objective deviation for evaluation and prioritization.

For objective (o_i), an objective deviation MAY be represented as:

[
d_{i,t}

D_i
\left(
X_t^{\mathrm{OSV}},
\mathcal{T}_i
\right)
]

where:

* (X_t^{\mathrm{OSV}}) is the committed OSV;
* (\mathcal{T}_i) is the objective target condition;
* (D_i) is a registered deterministic deviation function.

Objective deviation MUST NOT be treated as the canonical controller error.

Contract 007 owns control error.

Contract 006 transforms active objectives into profile-specific reference-state parameters before Contract 007 computes canonical control error.

18. Objective Priority

Every active objective MUST declare a priority.

Priority MAY include:

priority_class
priority_rank
criticality
mandatory_flag
governance_precedence_reference
tie_breaking_key

The initial priority classes are:

CRITICAL
HIGH
NORMAL
LOW
ADVISORY

Priority MUST NOT override governance.

A lower-priority objective MUST NOT invalidate a higher-precedence governance prohibition or obligation.

Priority resolution MUST be deterministic.

⸻

19. Objective Criticality

Criticality expresses the consequence of objective violation.

The initial criticality classes are:

CATASTROPHIC
SEVERE
MAJOR
MODERATE
MINOR
INFORMATIONAL

Criticality and priority are distinct.

A high-criticality objective may not always be the highest-priority objective if governance or context establishes another ordering.

The Objective Evaluation Profile MUST define how criticality contributes to priority resolution.

⸻

20. Objective Compatibility

Objectives are compatible when they can be simultaneously represented without mutually exclusive target conditions, prohibited authority expansion, or impossible requirements.

Every objective SHOULD declare compatibility and conflict groups.

Compatibility evaluation MUST consider:

* overlapping OSV fields;
* target-region intersection;
* temporal requirements;
* governance restrictions;
* objective class;
* required persistence;
* priority;
* criticality;
* profile feasibility metadata when available.

Contract 005 MAY determine that objectives are logically compatible or incompatible.

Contract 005 MUST NOT select the Operational Profile used to realize them.

⸻

21. Objective Conflict Types

The initial objective conflict vocabulary is:

TARGET_CONFLICT
BOUND_CONFLICT
PRIORITY_CONFLICT
TEMPORAL_CONFLICT
SCOPE_CONFLICT
GOVERNANCE_CONFLICT
RESOURCE_ASSUMPTION_CONFLICT
PERSISTENCE_CONFLICT
UNRESOLVED_CONFLICT

21.1 Target Conflict

Two objectives require mutually exclusive target conditions.

21.2 Bound Conflict

One objective’s required region violates another objective’s bound.

21.3 Priority Conflict

Two objectives claim equal precedence where simultaneous satisfaction is impossible.

21.4 Temporal Conflict

Objectives require incompatible timing, duration, or deadline behavior.

21.5 Scope Conflict

Objectives overlap with incompatible scope.

21.6 Governance Conflict

An objective or objective combination violates the Governance Snapshot.

21.7 Resource-Assumption Conflict

Objectives assume mutually incompatible capability or authority availability.

This classification does not permit Contract 005 to allocate runtime resources.

21.8 Persistence Conflict

One objective requires continued activation while another requires termination or replacement.

21.9 Unresolved Conflict

The available canonical information is insufficient to determine compatibility.

⸻

22. Objective Conflict Resolution

Objective conflicts MAY be resolved by:

* governance precedence;
* mandatory objective status;
* criticality;
* priority;
* narrower scope;
* explicit supersession;
* registered objective hierarchy;
* target-region intersection;
* deterministic objective reduction;
* deferral;
* escalation;
* exclusion of a lower-priority objective.

Conflict resolution MUST NOT:

* weaken governance;
* invent a new objective;
* modify OSV semantics;
* select a profile;
* create a control command;
* silently discard an objective.

Every excluded, deferred, restricted, or conflicted objective MUST remain represented in Objective Set provenance.

If a conflict cannot be resolved, the Objective Set MUST fail toward reduced supervisory authority.

⸻

A composite objective MAY combine registered objectives using:

ALL_OF

Composite objectives MUST preserve the identities and statuses of their component objectives.

A composite objective MUST NOT conceal an internal conflict.

The composition rule MUST be registered and deterministic.

24. Objective Persistence

An objective persistence policy defines whether an objective remains active across cycles.

The initial persistence classes are:

CYCLE_ONLY
UNTIL_SATISFIED

UNTIL_EXPIRED
UNTIL_REVOKED
UNTIL_SUPERSEDED
UNTIL_GOVERNANCE_CHANGE
EXPLICIT_RELEASE_REQUIRED

Persistence MUST NOT permit an objective to survive:

* governance prohibition;
* objective expiration;
* registry incompatibility;
* invalid system scope;
* explicit revocation;
* semantic supersession.

A continuing objective MUST be reevaluated against the current OSV and Governance Snapshot each cycle.

26. Objective Uncertainty

Uncertainty MUST NOT increase supervisory authority.

29. Objective Set Status

Every Objective Set MUST declare one status:

VALID
VALID_RESTRICTED
DEGRADED
CONFLICTED
INCOMPLETE
WITHHELD
FAULTED

29.1 Valid

All mandatory objective evaluations are resolved and the active objective combination is governance-eligible and internally compatible.

29.2 Valid Restricted

The Objective Set is valid but one or more objectives have been narrowed, deferred, or restricted by governance.

29.3 Degraded

The Objective Set may be used only under a registered degraded-objective policy.

29.4 Conflicted

One or more objective conflicts remain unresolved.

29.5 Incomplete

Mandatory objective inputs or evaluations are absent.

29.6 Withheld

The Objective Set was constructed but is not eligible for Profile Selector consumption.

29.7 Faulted

Objective evaluation failed due to integrity, compatibility, determinism, or authority failure.

A WITHHELD or FAULTED Objective Set MUST NOT be consumed by the Profile Selector.

A CONFLICTED or INCOMPLETE Objective Set MAY be consumed only if Contract 006 defines and registers a reduced-authority handling profile.

⸻

30. Atomic Commitment

Objective Set commitment MUST be atomic.

Before commitment, an Objective Set is not canonical.

After commitment:

* all included objectives are fixed;
* all objective statuses are fixed;
* the priority order is fixed;
* the conflict summary is fixed;
* the OSV reference is fixed;
* the Governance Snapshot reference is fixed;
* registry versions are fixed;
* the artifact hash is fixed;
* the Objective Set is immutable.

The Profile Selector MUST NOT read a partially constructed Objective Set.

⸻

31. Objective Set Binding

An Objective Set MUST be bound to:

* one supervisory cycle;
* one committed OSV;
* one committed Governance Snapshot;
* one Objective Evaluation Profile;
* one objective-registry version set;
* one effective-time context.

Objectives derived from different OSVs or Governance Snapshots MUST NOT be combined into one Objective Set.

33. Relationship to Operational Profiles

Supervisory Objectives state what condition is sought.

Operational Profiles define how those objectives and governance constraints are translated into:

* mode-specific expectations;
* allowed operating regions;
* profile permissions;
* reference-state parameters;
* controller limits;
* gain selections;
* transition eligibility.

The separation is:

Objective:
Preserve stability above a declared threshold.
Profile:
Use the profile-specific operating region, limits,
reference parameters, and controller configuration
appropriate to pursuing that objective.

An Objective MUST NOT contain profile-specific gains, commands, or reference-state construction logic.

⸻

34. Relationship to Reference State

Contract 005 declares target conditions and tolerances.

Contract 006 owns construction of the canonical Reference State.

An objective target condition MAY be qualitative, bounded, set-valued, or multidimensional.

Contract 006 MAY transform an active Objective Set into a profile-specific reference representation.

That transformation MUST preserve objective semantics and governance constraints.

⸻

35. Relationship to Governance

Governance defines what authority exists.

Objectives define what operational condition should be preserved or approached within that authority.

An objective MUST NOT:

* broaden permission;
* weaken prohibition;
* override a governance limit;
* remove an obligation;
* reinterpret a governance disposition;
* treat ALLOW as a requirement;
* treat absence of prohibition as objective eligibility unless permitted by the active governance model.

A governance-required objective remains declarative and must still be evaluated under this contract.

⸻

36. Relationship to Modes

Contract 010 owns modes, guards, transitions, resets, hysteresis, and dwell time.

A Supervisory Objective MAY indicate that stabilization is required.

It MUST NOT:

* activate Entry;
* activate Bloom;
* activate Harmonic;
* activate Fracture;
* activate Stabilize;
* evaluate a mode guard;
* execute a reset map.

Mode-dependent objective rules MAY be added after Contract 010 is adopted, provided they do not transfer mode-selection authority to Contract 005.

⸻

37. Failure Principle

Objective evaluation MUST fail toward reduced supervisory authority.

When OSV validity, governance validity, objective applicability, eligibility, compatibility, priority, uncertainty treatment, registry compatibility, or provenance is unresolved, the Objective Evaluator MUST NOT activate a more permissive or expansive objective configuration.

Failure MAY produce:

VALID_RESTRICTED
DEGRADED
CONFLICTED
INCOMPLETE
WITHHELD
FAULTED

Missing or uncertain objective information MUST NOT create a new permission or widen the operating region.

⸻

40. Objective Updates During a Cycle

A committed Objective Set is immutable for its supervisory cycle.

An objective update received after commitment MUST NOT mutate the active Objective Set.

A conforming implementation MAY:

* apply the update during the next cycle;
* terminate the current cycle;
* initiate an explicitly authorized reevaluation cycle;
* withhold profile selection pending reevaluation.

The selected behavior MUST be defined by a registered Objective Update Policy.

⸻

41. Objective Supersession

An objective MAY supersede another objective only through an explicit registered relationship.

Supersession MUST preserve:

superseding_objective_id
superseded_objective_id

scope

authority_reference
provenance_id

Supersession MUST NOT erase the prior objective or its historical evaluation.

42. Security and Integrity

Objective interfaces SHOULD support:

* registry integrity;
* objective-source authentication;
* artifact signing;
* tamper-evident storage;
* replay protection;
* schema validation;
* version verification;
* sensitivity labeling;
* least-privilege evaluator execution.

A failed integrity check MUST NOT produce a VALID Objective Set.

⸻

43. Sensitive Objectives

Objectives MAY contain sensitive operational conditions.

An objective sensitivity profile SHOULD define:

* permitted consumers;
* disclosure restrictions;
* storage requirements;
* retention limits;
* redaction behavior;
* audit requirements.

Redaction MUST NOT conceal an active requirement, prohibition interaction, priority, or enforcement-relevant target from the Profile Selector.

A redacted objective MAY expose a canonical identifier, target class, and required handling without exposing protected descriptive text.

44. Architectural Prohibitions

A conforming Objective implementation MUST NOT:

* consume raw telemetry;
* consume Estimated State directly;
* modify the committed OSV;
* modify the Governance Snapshot;
* activate a governance-prohibited objective;
* create permissions;
* weaken prohibitions;
* define controller gains;
* construct the canonical Reference State;
* select an Operational Profile;
* calculate canonical control error;
* issue control commands;
* issue inference commands;
* execute runtime instructions;

48. Non-Goals

This contract does not define:

* OSV field semantics;
* governance content;
* laws or policies;
* operational-profile parameters;
* profile-selection algorithms;
* Reference State construction;
* controller gains;
* controller mathematics;
* inference-command schemas;
* execution behavior;
* mode guards;
* reset maps;
* verification proofs;
* task-level goals or generated content.

Task goals and Supervisory Objectives are not necessarily equivalent.

A task goal describes work the supervised system is attempting to perform.

A Supervisory Objective describes the operational condition SACS is attempting to preserve while that work occurs.
