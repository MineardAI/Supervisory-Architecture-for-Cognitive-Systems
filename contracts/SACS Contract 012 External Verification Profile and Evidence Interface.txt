* verifiable claims;

Contract 012 converts immutable SACS constitutional history into a bounded, deterministic, externally consumable verification submission.

## Constitutional Identity

Contract 012 is the constitutional boundary between internally committed SACS history and externally issued verification status.

Contract 012 enables external verification.

## Section 3 â€” Verification Scope and Applicability

### Purpose

Section 3 defines when Contract 012 applies, what may become a verification subject, what kinds of claims may be submitted, and the constitutional limits of every verification scope.

Contract 012 SHALL operate only when an explicitly authorized verification purpose, verification subject, Verification Profile, and external verification authority have been identified.

The existence of a committed historical artifact does not by itself require external verification.

The existence of a Verification Profile does not by itself authorize every artifact to be submitted under that profile.

Applicability SHALL be established explicitly.

---

# Constitutional Question

Section 3 answers:

> **Which committed SACS artifacts and claims are eligible for external verification under one declared Verification Profile, and what exact boundaries constrain that verification?**

The answer SHALL be determined without:

* altering the verification subject;
* inferring unstated claims;
* extending verification to unrelated artifacts;
* treating historical commitment as verification;
* treating profile availability as submission authorization.

---

# Verification Applicability

Contract 012 applies only when all of the following exist:

1. one eligible immutable verification subject;
2. one explicit verification purpose;
3. one authorized Verification Profile;
4. one applicable Verification Requirement Set;
5. one recognized external verification authority;
6. one declared verification scope;
7. one cycle-bound verification configuration;
8. sufficient evidence-resolution authority to attempt package construction.

If any mandatory applicability condition is absent, unresolved, incompatible, or unauthorized, Contract 012 SHALL NOT construct a canonical Verification Submission.

The result SHALL be an explicit verification-preparation failure.

---

# Subject Scope and Evidence Scope

Verification subject scope and evidence scope SHALL remain distinct.

The verification subject is the artifact, transformation, implementation, or property being evaluated.

The evidence scope is the exact set of artifacts and supporting materials permitted or required to evaluate the claim.

Formally:

[
\text{Verification Subject}
\neq
\text{Verification Evidence Set}
]

The evidence scope MAY be broader than the direct subject scope.

It SHALL NOT be narrower than the minimum evidence required by the Verification Profile.

# Failure Conditions

* verifier identity is unresolved;
* verifier authorization is absent;
* transmission integrity cannot be established;

The NullBlade-to-Contract 012 boundary SHALL fail when:

* the result signature is invalid;
* submission identity is missing or conflicting;
* claim identity is unknown;

# SACS Contract 012 â€” External Verification Profile and Evidence Interface

## Section 6 â€” Verifiable Claim

### Purpose

Section 6 defines the canonical constitutional object that external verification evaluates.

A `Verifiable Claim` is an explicit, bounded, immutable assertion concerning one eligible verification subject.

Every external verification operation under Contract 012 SHALL evaluate one or more Verifiable Claims.

No Verification Profile, evidence package, submission, external result, or verification-status record may exist canonically without resolving to at least one Verifiable Claim.

The purpose of this section is to ensure that external verification always answers a precise question rather than issuing general, implied, or unbounded assurance.

---

# Constitutional Question

Section 6 answers:

> **What exact assertion concerning an eligible verification subject may be prepared for external verification?**

A Verifiable Claim SHALL state:

* what property is asserted;
* which subject is covered;
* which scope applies;
* which versions apply;
* which conditions apply;
* which assumptions are relied upon;
* which exclusions apply;
* which evidence classes may support evaluation;
* what kind of result may be returned.

A claim that cannot answer these questions SHALL NOT become canonical.

---

# Constitutional Identity

The defining identity of a Verifiable Claim is:

> **A Verifiable Claim is one immutable, explicitly scoped, independently identifiable assertion whose meaning, subject, assumptions, exclusions, and verification boundaries are fully determined before external verification begins.**

A claim is not:

* a verification result;
* an evidence package;
* an implementation guarantee;
* a general statement of trust;
* a governance decision;
* a constitutional amendment;
* a prediction of verifier outcome.

* assign canonical claim identity;
* bind a claim to one eligible verification subject;
* bind declared assumptions and exclusions;

## Section 10 â€” Verification Scope and Applicability

### Purpose

Section 10 defines the constitutional gate that determines whether a proposed external verification operation may proceed.

This section evaluates whether one exact:

* verification subject;

Applicability is not:

* claim verification;
* evidence sufficiency judgment by the external verifier;
* verifier success;
* implementation certification;
* historical certification;
* governance approval;

# Not Applicable

`NOT_APPLICABLE` means the verification request does not meaningfully apply to the presented subject, claim, scope, or purpose.

Examples include:

* a runtime-behavior claim applied to a schema-only artifact;

* no result schema can represent the required outcome;
* the subject type is not yet supported by Contract 012.

* evidence and privacy-policy incompatibility;

Examples include:

* unresolved implementation hash;
* missing environment identity;
* unavailable registry snapshot;
* unresolved verifier credential;
* ambiguous temporal boundary;
* unresolved profile status;
* incomplete version binding;
* unresolved requirement conflict.

Examples include:

* use of an unauthorized verifier;
* submission of prohibited evidence;
