# SACS Contract 012 — External Verification Profile and Evidence Interface

## Section 22 — Fundamental Inter-Constitutional Trust Exchange Invariant

### Purpose

Section 22 establishes the Fundamental Inter-Constitutional Trust Exchange Invariant governing every verification exchange performed under Contract 012.

The Fundamental Invariant expresses the single constitutional law that every preceding section exists to preserve.

Sections 1 through 21 define constitutional authority, verification preparation, submission, commitment, inter-constitutional trust exchange, historical preservation, replay, and audit.

None of those activities constitute the purpose of Contract 012.

Their collective purpose is to preserve one immutable constitutional property.

---

# Constitutional Identity

The defining constitutional identity of Contract 012 is:

> **Independent constitutional systems MAY exchange verification responsibilities without surrendering constitutional sovereignty, ownership, historical authority, or commitment authority.**

Trust is therefore established through:

* explicit constitutional delegation;
* bounded responsibility;
* immutable commitments;
* preserved authority;
* preserved provenance;
* preserved custody;
* deterministic historical traceability.

Trust SHALL NOT require constitutional authority to cross constitutional boundaries.

---

# Fundamental Constitutional Law

For every Committed Verification Submission admitted to the external-verification boundary:

Exactly one constitutionally bounded verification exchange SHALL exist.

That exchange SHALL terminate in exactly one of the following:

* one immutable **Committed Verification Exchange Record**; or
* one immutable **Committed Verification Exchange Failure**.

Both SHALL never exist simultaneously.

Neither SHALL ever be absent.

Formally:

```text
For every Verification Exchange Identity:

Count(Committed Verification Exchange Record)

+

Count(Committed Verification Exchange Failure)

=

1
```

This invariant SHALL hold independently of:

* implementation architecture;
* execution platform;
* communication protocol;
* storage technology;
* verification methodology;
* cryptographic implementation;
* deployment environment.

# Constitutional Sovereignty

Every verification exchange SHALL preserve the constitutional sovereignty of all participating constitutional systems.

Accordingly:

* the originating constitutional system SHALL remain sovereign over its constitutional history;
* the external verification authority SHALL remain sovereign over its verification judgment;
* constitutional authority SHALL never migrate across constitutional boundaries;
* constitutional ownership SHALL never transfer through verification;
* commitment authority SHALL remain exclusive to the constitutional system committing its own historical record.

No constitutional system SHALL commit historical artifacts on behalf of another.

Every verification exchange SHALL preserve the separation of constitutional systems.

Accordingly:

SACS SHALL prepare, submit, admit, certify, commit, preserve, replay, and audit its own constitutional artifacts.

Neither constitutional system SHALL:

* reinterpret the other's sovereign decisions;
* modify the other's committed history;
* assume the other's constitutional authority;
* conceal unfavorable outcomes;
* broaden delegated responsibility beyond its constitutional scope.

Trust SHALL therefore arise from preserved constitutional independence rather than merged constitutional authority.
