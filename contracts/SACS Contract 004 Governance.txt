1. Purpose

This contract defines the canonical governance model for SACS.

It establishes:

* governance authority;
* external governance-source requirements;
* governance adapters;
* immutable Governance Snapshots;
* permissions;
* prohibitions;
* obligations;
* limits;
* execution eligibility;
* state-conditional constraints;
* conflict resolution;
* precedence;
* validity;
* failure behavior;
* provenance;
* deterministic governance evaluation;
* downstream enforcement requirements.

Governance defines the authority within which supervision may operate.

Governance does not determine the system’s desired operating condition, select an operational profile, calculate control error, generate control commands, or execute runtime actions.

⸻

2. Normative Language

The key words MUST, MUST NOT, REQUIRED, SHALL, SHALL NOT, SHOULD, SHOULD NOT, RECOMMENDED, MAY, and OPTIONAL are normative conformance terms as defined by Contract 000.

⸻

3. Governance Identity

Governance is the canonical representation of externally supplied constraints, permissions, prohibitions, obligations, limits, and execution eligibility applicable to a SACS supervisory cycle.

The defining identity of Governance is:

Governance determines what supervisory and execution authority exists, not what operational outcome should be pursued.

Governance MAY constrain:

* supervisory objectives;
* operational-profile eligibility;
* reference-state bounds;
* controller output space;
* inference-command translation;
* tool access;
* model-routing authority;
* adaptive-update authority;
* memory-write authority;
* data-access authority;
* execution eligibility;
* escalation requirements;
* audit requirements.

Governance MUST NOT:

* estimate operational state;
* redefine OSV fields;
* select supervisory objectives;
* activate operational profiles;
* compute controller outputs;
* generate task content;
* directly perform execution;
* alter observations;
* mutate the committed OSV.

⸻

4. Separation of Governance Authority

SACS consumes governance.

SACS does not author foundational governance content.

A conforming implementation MUST distinguish:

External Governance Authority
        ↓
Governance Source Artifact
        ↓
Governance Adapter
        ↓
Canonical Governance Snapshot
        ↓
SACS Supervisory Consumers

The External Governance Authority owns the originating policy, rule, permission, prohibition, or constraint semantics.

The Governance Adapter owns transformation into the canonical SACS governance representation.

SACS owns application of the resulting Governance Snapshot within the supervisory architecture.

The Governance Adapter MUST NOT create authority absent from the external source.

SACS MUST NOT modify, weaken, expand, reinterpret, or silently substitute the Governance Snapshot.

9. Governance Rule Classes

The initial governance rule classes are:

PERMISSION
PROHIBITION
OBLIGATION
LIMIT
ELIGIBILITY
REQUIREMENT
ESCALATION
AUDIT
RETENTION
DISCLOSURE
REDUCTION

9.1 Permission

A PERMISSION declares that an action or authority MAY be exercised when all applicable conditions are satisfied.

Permission does not require that the action occur.

9.2 Prohibition

A PROHIBITION declares that an action, authority, transition, or execution MUST NOT occur under the applicable scope.

A prohibition overrides a permission of equal or lower precedence.

9.3 Obligation

An OBLIGATION declares that a required action, record, notification, review, or constraint MUST be satisfied.

Failure to satisfy an obligation MUST produce an explicit governance fault or restricted disposition.

9.4 Limit

A LIMIT bounds a value, rate, duration, magnitude, count, resource, or authority.

Examples include:

* maximum tool-call count;
* maximum adaptive-update magnitude;
* maximum model temperature;
* maximum memory-retention duration;
* maximum execution duration;
* maximum recursion allowance.

9.5 Eligibility

An ELIGIBILITY rule declares whether a governed capability, profile, command class, tool, model, or execution path may be considered.

Eligibility does not activate that capability.

9.6 Requirement

A REQUIREMENT defines a prerequisite that MUST be satisfied before a governed action may proceed.

9.7 Escalation

An ESCALATION rule requires referral to another authority, human reviewer, safety process, or external system.

9.8 Audit

An AUDIT rule defines required recording, evidence, traceability, or reporting.

9.9 Retention

A RETENTION rule constrains how long data, state, logs, or artifacts may or must be retained.

9.10 Disclosure

A DISCLOSURE rule defines required or prohibited disclosure of system status, limitations, provenance, or decision basis.

9.11 Reduction

A REDUCTION rule requires decreased execution authority under specified conditions.

11. Governance Conditions

A governance rule MAY be unconditional or conditional.

A conditional rule MAY reference:

* committed OSV fields;
* committed OSV validity states;
* committed OSV uncertainty;
* declared system identity;
* deployment context;
* capability profile;
* external authorization state;
* active objective identifiers;
* candidate profile identifiers;
* candidate control-command classes;
* candidate inference-command classes;
* execution target;
* time;
* jurisdiction;

* uncommitted telemetry;
* estimator-local state;
* mutable OSV construction state;
* hidden model activations unless represented in the committed OSV;
* unregistered context.

Where operational state is required, governance MUST reference only the committed OSV.

13. Snapshot Binding

A Governance Snapshot MUST be bound to:

* one supervisory cycle;
* one committed OSV;
* one effective-time context;
* one declared governance-source set;
* one compatibility profile;
* one canonical rule set.

The same source governance MAY produce different snapshots when:

* the committed OSV changes;
* effective time changes;
* scope changes;
* the deployment context changes;
* a rule is revoked;
* a higher-precedence rule becomes applicable;
* the governance source set changes.

A snapshot MUST NOT be retroactively modified when these conditions change.

⸻

14. Snapshot Status

Every Governance Snapshot MUST declare one status:

VALID
VALID_RESTRICTED
DEGRADED
INCOMPLETE
WITHHELD
FAULTED
REVOKED

14.1 VALID

All mandatory governance sources, rules, signatures, versions, and applicability conditions are resolved.

14.2 VALID_RESTRICTED

The snapshot is valid but permits only reduced authority due to applicable governance constraints.

14.3 DEGRADED

The snapshot is usable only under an explicitly registered degraded-governance policy.

14.4 INCOMPLETE

One or more required governance inputs are unresolved.

14.5 WITHHELD

The snapshot was constructed but is not eligible for downstream use.

14.6 FAULTED

Construction failed because integrity, compatibility, precedence, or authority could not be resolved.

14.7 REVOKED

The governing authority revoked the snapshot or a mandatory source artifact.

A WITHHELD, FAULTED, or REVOKED snapshot MUST NOT authorize execution.

⸻

15. Permissions Are Not Defaults

Permission MUST be explicit.

The absence of a prohibition MUST NOT be interpreted as permission.

The absence of a permission MUST NOT be interpreted as prohibition unless the active governance profile declares a closed-world policy.

Every Governance Snapshot MUST declare one authority model:

CLOSED_WORLD
OPEN_WORLD_RESTRICTED
HYBRID

15.1 Closed-World Governance

Only explicitly permitted authority is available.

15.2 Open-World Restricted Governance

Authority is available unless explicitly prohibited, but only within declared baseline limits and capability bounds.

15.3 Hybrid Governance

Different capability classes use different authority models.

The selected model MUST be explicit, registered, versioned, and deterministic.

⸻

16. Governance Precedence

Governance rules MUST be resolved using an explicit precedence model.

The initial precedence dimensions are:

1. revocation
2. mandatory prohibition
3. mandatory obligation
4. safety or legal limit
5. explicit eligibility restriction
6. scoped permission
7. default governance profile

This ordering is illustrative until adopted through a Governance Precedence Profile.

A precedence profile MUST define:

precedence_profile_id
rule_class_order
authority_priority_order
specificity_rules
scope_rules
time_rules
exception_rules
tie_breaking_rules
unresolved_conflict_behavior
schema_version
registry_version

No rule conflict may be resolved by undocumented implementation order.

⸻

17. Conflict Resolution

A governance conflict exists when two applicable rules produce incompatible effects over the same subject, resource, capability, or action.

A conforming implementation MUST distinguish:

DIRECT_CONFLICT
SCOPE_CONFLICT
AUTHORITY_CONFLICT
TEMPORAL_CONFLICT
VERSION_CONFLICT
EXCEPTION_CONFLICT
UNRESOLVED_CONFLICT

Conflicts MUST be preserved in provenance.

A conflict MAY be resolved by:

* higher authority;
* explicit rule priority;
* narrower scope;
* later valid supersession;
* mandatory prohibition precedence;
* explicit exception relationship;
* registered tie-break rule.

If conflict remains unresolved, the snapshot MUST fail toward reduced authority.

An unresolved conflict MUST NOT produce increased permission.

⸻

18. Governance Disposition Vocabulary

Contract 004 owns governance disposition semantics.

Contract 009 owns execution disposition semantics.

The initial governance disposition vocabulary is:

BLOCK
STABILIZE_REQUIRED
DEFER
RESTRICT
ALLOW
ESCALATE
REVIEW_REQUIRED

18.1 BLOCK

The governed action or authority is prohibited.

No downstream component may override BLOCK.

18.2 STABILIZE_REQUIRED

The current authority is insufficient for nominal continuation, and the supervisory path MUST move toward a stabilization-compatible condition defined by later contracts.

This disposition does not itself select a profile or issue a control command.

18.3 DEFER

The governed action is not currently authorized but MAY be reconsidered when specified evidence or conditions become available.

18.4 RESTRICT

The governed action MAY proceed only within explicitly declared reduced limits.

18.5 ALLOW

The governed action is eligible within all applicable limits and requirements.

ALLOW does not require execution.

18.6 ESCALATE

The governed action requires referral to a declared external or human authority.

18.7 REVIEW_REQUIRED

The governed action requires formal review before authorization.

The default conservative ordering is:

BLOCK
    >
STABILIZE_REQUIRED
    >
ESCALATE
    >
REVIEW_REQUIRED
    >
DEFER
    >
RESTRICT
    >
ALLOW

The exact precedence MUST be declared by the active Governance Disposition Profile.

22. Controller Constraint Boundary

Contract 007 defines controller behavior.

The controller MUST compute only within the action space permitted by the active Governance Snapshot.

Let:

[
\mathcal{G}_t

\operatorname{GovernanceSnapshot}(t)
]

and let:

[
\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{allowed}}(\mathcal{G}_t)
]

denote the control-command set permitted by governance.

Then every candidate controller output (u_t) MUST satisfy:

[
u_t
\in
\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{allowed}}(\mathcal{G}_t)
]

A candidate outside the allowed set MUST be rejected, restricted, deferred, escalated, or blocked according to the active governance rules.

The controller MUST NOT reinterpret the Governance Snapshot.

⸻

23. Inference-Control Constraint Boundary

Contract 008 defines translation from control commands to inference commands.

Inference Control MUST NOT broaden governance authority.

If a control command permits a bounded instruction, translation MUST NOT produce a stronger instruction.

Every translated command MUST retain:

governance_snapshot_id
governance_evaluation_id
applicable_limit_ids
applicable_prohibition_ids
required_obligation_ids

Translation failure MUST fail toward reduced authority.

⸻

24. Execution-Gate Boundary

Contract 009 defines execution dispositions and execution receipts.

The Execution Gate MUST independently enforce the Governance Snapshot applicable to the cycle.

The Execution Gate MUST reject any instruction that:

* lacks a valid Governance Snapshot reference;
* references a revoked snapshot;
* exceeds a governance limit;
* violates a prohibition;
* lacks required approval;
* lacks required evidence;
* violates scope;
* uses an incompatible governance version;
* has been broadened during translation.

Execution enforcement is defense in depth.

It does not authorize earlier layers to ignore governance.

⸻

25. Governance Validity

A governance rule or snapshot MAY be valid only within a declared:

* time interval;
* jurisdiction;
* deployment;
* model;
* tool;
* user role;
* data class;
* capability profile;
* system identity;
* session;
* supervisory cycle;
* operational condition.

Validity MUST be evaluated before commitment.

Expired governance MUST NOT be treated as current.

Future governance MUST NOT be applied early.

Unknown validity MUST fail toward reduced authority.

⸻

26. Revocation

Governance authorities MUST be able to revoke governance artifacts according to their registered revocation policy.

Revocation MUST produce:

revocation_id
governance_authority_id
revoked_artifact_id
revoked_rule_ids
effective_time
reason_code
replacement_artifact_id
signature
provenance_id

A revocation MUST NOT mutate an earlier committed Governance Snapshot.

A later cycle MUST construct a new snapshot reflecting the revocation.

If immediate revocation enforcement is required between cycles, the runtime MUST terminate, pause, or enter a reduced-authority path defined by the active governance profile.

⸻

27. Governance Updates During a Cycle

A committed Governance Snapshot is immutable for its supervisory cycle.

A governance update received after snapshot commitment MUST NOT modify that snapshot.

A conforming implementation MAY:

* apply the update to the next cycle;
* terminate the current cycle;
* initiate an emergency governance cycle;
* block execution pending new snapshot commitment.

The selected behavior MUST be defined by a registered Governance Update Policy.

⸻

28. Missing Governance

Required governance that is missing, unavailable, invalid, expired, revoked, incompatible, or unauthenticated MUST NOT be treated as permission.

A conforming implementation MUST produce one of:

VALID_RESTRICTED
INCOMPLETE
WITHHELD
FAULTED

according to the active governance profile.

Missing governance MUST fail toward reduced authority.

⸻

29. Governance Uncertainty

Governance content SHOULD be explicit rather than probabilistic.

A governance source MAY include uncertainty about applicability, interpretation, authority, or jurisdiction.

Such uncertainty MUST be preserved.

Probabilistic governance MUST NOT directly authorize execution unless a later profile defines a deterministic normalization threshold and conservative resolution rule.

Unknown governance applicability MUST NOT increase authority.

31. Security and Integrity

Governance interfaces SHOULD support:

* authority authentication;
* artifact signing;
* signature verification;
* replay protection;
* tamper-evident storage;
* sequence validation;
* revocation checking;
* trust-domain separation;
* sensitivity labeling;
* least-privilege adapter execution;
* independent execution-gate verification.

A failed integrity check MUST NOT produce a VALID Governance Snapshot.

⸻

32. Privacy and Confidential Governance

Governance artifacts MAY contain confidential or sensitive constraints.

A governance sensitivity profile SHOULD define:

* permitted consumers;
* disclosure restrictions;
* storage requirements;
* retention rules;
* redaction rules;
* audit requirements.

Redaction MUST NOT hide the existence of an applicable prohibition or required restriction from a downstream enforcement component.

A redacted snapshot MAY expose a constraint identifier and enforcement effect without exposing protected policy text.

⸻

33. Determinism

For identical:

committed OSV
governance source artifacts
authority registrations
adapter versions
precedence profile
conflict-resolution profile
compatibility profile
registry versions
effective-time context

a conforming implementation MUST produce the same canonical Governance Snapshot.

Undeclared implementation-order dependence is prohibited.

Randomized policy resolution is prohibited in the canonical governance path.

⸻

34. Failure Principle

Governance MUST fail toward reduced authority.

When authority, identity, integrity, applicability, validity, revocation, precedence, compatibility, provenance, or scope is uncertain, governance MUST NOT increase execution freedom.

The conservative governance ordering is:

BLOCK
    >
STABILIZE_REQUIRED
    >
ESCALATE
    >
REVIEW_REQUIRED
    >
DEFER
    >
RESTRICT
    >
ALLOW

This ordering expresses authority conservatism.

It does not imply semantic equivalence between dispositions.

⸻

35. Architectural Prohibitions

A conforming Governance implementation MUST NOT:

* author foundational governance on behalf of an external authority;
* mutate a committed Governance Snapshot;
* consume raw telemetry instead of the committed OSV;
* modify the committed OSV;
* infer permission from silence unless explicitly allowed by the authority model;
* suppress prohibitions;
* broaden permissions;
* extend validity without authority;
* ignore revocation;
* silently resolve conflicts;
* silently merge incompatible versions;
* select supervisory objectives;
* activate operational profiles;
* compute controller actions;
* issue inference commands;
* execute runtime instructions;
* permit downstream layers to override BLOCK;
* treat adapter health telemetry as governance content;
* claim external verification based solely on internal governance evaluation.

38. Assumptions Discharged

This contract discharges the Contract 000 assumption that governance snapshots are immutable during a supervisory cycle by defining:

* snapshot construction;
* atomic commitment;
* cycle binding;

* external governance authorities can authenticate their artifacts;
* external governance semantics can be represented through registered adapters;
* downstream components can enforce governance constraints;
* the Execution Gate can reject unauthorized instructions.
